# Known limitations

- **Levels and headroom:** EG13 does not yet manage gain between voices and parts. Sixteen loud voices can peak well above full scale (5 to 13 times, measured on chords). Some Filter and Shaper settings are much louder than their input (measured with a half-scale saw):
  - Several Classic Filters types at full Resonance peak at about 4.5, about +13 dB above full scale.
  - The EQ at its extremes (+18 dB with a loud input) clips inside, and the output can briefly ring to about 4 times full scale.
  - The Ring Mod / Vocoder at its defaults is 4.6 dB louder than its input (peak 1.24 from 0.5), and up to 12.3 dB louder at Algorithm *Fold* (peak 2.0). Its output never exceeds Warps' own 16-bit range (3.2 times full scale).
  - At their defaults the filters and the EQ keep the level (within 0.05 dB).

  Keep the master level down. A later version adds gain staging and a soft clipper on the master output.
- **Resonator velocity:** a new note's velocity rescales the whole Resonator output, including voices still ringing, which can click (to be fixed in a later version).
- **Modal Synth input:** the firmware's noise gate on the external input is not ported.
- **Part B in Single mode** still runs and uses CPU, so it can feed Part A.
- **Ring Mod / Vocoder noise carrier:** with Osc Shape *Saw / Noise* in the vocoder range, the noise comes from a random generator shared by the voices on one audio thread, so two renders of the same notes are not sample-identical.
- **CPU:** sixteen voices (two parts of eight) of the heaviest chain with a mono Source, Macro Oscillator → Analog Filter → Vocoder, use 49.0 % of one Apple M1 core when the Ring Mod / Vocoder's Carrier and Modulator are the internal oscillator or the voice. That is within the budget of half a core. With a side input carrying a signal the same chain is **over** that budget: 50.3 % with one (Modulator *Input*) and 51.5 % with two (Carrier *Source AUX*, Modulator *Input*). With a stereo Source (Modal Synth, Resonator with Spread, a stereo input), every Filter and Shaper runs twice, and the heaviest stereo chain uses 88.2 % (Modal Synth → Analog Filter → Vocoder). Engines that run at their own sample rate save work by converting a side input only while it carries a signal, and the right channel only while it differs from the left; this changes nothing you can hear. When a voice turns stereo, or a side input starts after silence, the conversion restarts with a short burst of extra work in that one block. EG13 checks each conversion path when the plugin is prepared and skips only where the restarted conversion is proven identical; should a restart still come back out of step while playing, that engine restarts all its conversions at once, which leaves a one-off silent gap about as long as its latency (a few milliseconds) and keeps it in time with the rest of the voice; a held note keeps sounding after the gap. It then converts every channel until the plugin is prepared again. See the README for the measured figures.
- **Classic Filters Model** changes restart the filter and can click on a sounding note.
